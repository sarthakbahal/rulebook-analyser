# Hostel Rules

> **Corpus status:** This file is a synthetic companion to `academic_handbook.pdf`, derived in part from the uploaded 2026-2027 Mind and Hand Book. It is designed for question-answering and contradiction-detection testing. It is **not** an official MIT residence handbook.

## 1. Residence Conduct, Access, and Curfew

### 1.1 General residence expectations

Residents are expected to treat other residents, visitors, Institute property, and shared spaces with respect. Residence halls are part of the Institute community, so the general expectations of professionalism, personal integrity, respect for others, and care for Institute property apply in residential settings.

Students must comply with Institute rules, applicable laws, and any posted residence-hall requirements. Improper use of Institute property includes vandalism, damage or destruction, entering restricted or private areas without authorization, engaging in illegal activity, or using Institute property in a manner that violates other Institute policies.

### 1.2 Residence access and identification

MIT ID cards are required to gain access to Institute residence halls. Students must not give their physical MIT ID card, Mobile ID, or Mobile ID data to another person. Residents should not enter locked, posted, private, or otherwise unauthorized areas. Examples of unauthorized areas discussed in the source handbook include restricted rooftops and other locations where access is prohibited.

### 1.3 Fire and emergency evacuation

When a fire alarm is activated in a residence hall, residents are expected to evacuate by the safest and closest exit to the designated meeting point. Residents should evacuate immediately rather than attempting to fight a fire. Failure to evacuate may expose a student to government penalties and Institute disciplinary action.

Residents should also follow applicable fire-safety requirements, including restrictions on unsafe electrical equipment and other prohibited practices described in Institute fire-safety policies.

### 1.4 Curfew and late entry

The residential curfew for this test corpus is **10:00 PM**.

**Entering the hostel after 10:00 PM incurs an automated fine of $50.**

Residents returning after curfew must use the approved entry point and present valid identification or access credentials when requested. Late-entry records may be maintained for residential safety and administrative purposes.

### 1.5 Events and shared spaces

Residence-hall events must follow the Institute's event-planning and space-use expectations. Alcohol-related events are subject to the separate alcohol policy and event protocols. Residents should avoid conduct that creates a disturbance, endangers people or property, or interferes with the ordinary activities of the Institute or other residents.

## 2. Residence Safety and Community Standards

### 2.1 Smoking and prohibited substances

Smoking is restricted in Institute residence halls under applicable residence-hall policies. The Institute's broader alcohol and prohibited-substance rules continue to apply in housing. Prohibited substances include illegal drugs and other substances that are considered dangerous or detrimental even when they may not be illegal. Students must not permit prohibited substances to be used in their residence.

### 2.2 Security

Students should help maintain a secure residential environment. Do not prop open controlled-access doors, share access credentials, or enter spaces for which you do not have permission. Students are encouraged to report urgent safety concerns to MIT Police and appropriate residential staff.

### 2.3 Rooftops and restricted areas

Unauthorized access to rooftops, locked areas, construction sites, mechanical spaces, or other restricted Institute property is prohibited. Calling an activity a "hack" does not turn an otherwise unlawful or unsafe act into permitted conduct. The source handbook emphasizes safety, respect for people and property, and leaving spaces as they were found.

### 2.4 Community well-being

MIT expects students not to engage in behavior that endangers their own sustained effectiveness or seriously affects their physical or mental health, safety, welfare, academic well-being, professional obligations, or the well-being of others. Residential decisions may therefore take the broader community's safety and welfare into account.

## 3. Leave, Temporary Absence, and Medical Situations

### 3.1 General leave reasons

Students may need time away from the Institute for many reasons. The source handbook identifies personal or medical issues that interfere with academics, professional experiences such as internships, family or community commitments, or a desire to take a break as possible reasons for leave.

Students should consult the appropriate Institute leave resources for the applicable process. Undergraduate and graduate medical leave are handled through their respective Institute resources.

### 3.2 Hospitalization attendance exception

**In case of hospitalization, attendance up to 20% can be waived by the Dean upon submission of medical certificates, allowing eligibility at 55% attendance.**

This sentence is intentionally planted in the test corpus and conflicts with the attendance statement on page 4 of `academic_handbook.pdf`. The contradiction is preserved so that question-answering systems can be evaluated on whether they recognize conflicting rules rather than merging them into one answer.

### 3.3 Misconduct while on leave

Misconduct by a student on leave that comes to the Institute's attention may be a basis for disciplinary action. Possible consequences include temporary or permanent denial of a request to return from leave or referral to the Committee on Discipline.

### 3.4 Leave procedures

For an actual leave request, students should use the official Institute leave resources rather than rely on the simplified residence rules in this synthetic test corpus. The source handbook points students to the Student Support Services leave information for undergraduate medical leave and to the Office of Graduate Education resources for graduate medical leave.

## 4. Emergencies, Missing Students, and Reporting

### 4.1 Emergency contacts

Students should keep emergency contact information current and should review their emergency contact, term address, and home address information through WebSIS as needed.

### 4.2 Missing-person concerns

If a student cannot be found and there is a concern that the person may be missing or in danger, the source handbook instructs community members to immediately contact MIT Police. Students should also notify the Head of House or Dean on Call when appropriate.

### 4.3 Missing student notification

Students living in on-campus housing, including approved FSILG housing, may identify an individual whom MIT can contact after the student is determined to be missing. Missing Person Emergency Contact information can be supplied through WebSIS. Except as permitted or required by law, such information is intended to be kept confidential and accessible only to authorized campus officials, with law-enforcement disclosure allowed in furtherance of a missing-person investigation.

## 5. Enforcement and Late-Entry Penalties

### 5.1 First late entry

**First-time late entry after 10:00 PM will result in a written warning only; fines apply strictly from the second offense.**

This statement is intentionally inconsistent with Section 1.4. Section 1.4 states that an automated $50 fine applies after 10:00 PM, while this section states that the first offense receives only a written warning. This contradiction is included for benchmark testing.

### 5.2 Repeated or serious violations

Repeated conduct, unsafe behavior, property damage, unauthorized access, or other policy violations may be referred through applicable Institute conduct procedures. Depending on the conduct, outcomes may include warnings, probation, suspension, expulsion, loss of housing privileges, educational requirements, or other sanctions.

### 5.3 Good-faith help seeking

The source handbook emphasizes that seeking medical assistance during certain emergencies can be a mitigating factor. The Good Samaritan Amnesty Policy is intended to reduce barriers to seeking help in alcohol- or prohibited-substance-related emergencies and other situations covered by the policy. Its protections do not eliminate potential action for other serious violations.
